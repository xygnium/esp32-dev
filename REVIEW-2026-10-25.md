# Review: sensor-probe, ambient/WIRING.md, ambient/FIRMWARE.md (2026-10-25)

Report only; nothing else was edited. Findings are labelled **wrong**, **unverified** or **suggestion** and ranked by severity.

How it was checked:
- Thermistor numbers were recomputed against `/home/mike/sandbox/thermistor-103JL1A/10k-2.A.txt`.
- The Littelfuse PDF could not be read (no PDF tools installed), so its time-constant and dissipation figures are still unchecked.
- The BMP388 and SHT45 code was checked against my knowledge of the datasheets, not the datasheets themselves.

Two of the author's known unknowns are now settled:
- **~33 kΩ at 0 °C:** confirmed. The table gives 32,650 Ω.
- **BMP388 and SHT45 math:** matches the datasheets as I know them (see the last section).

## Highest severity

1. **Wrong: FIRMWARE.md Table 1, "GPIO25 high only during a conversion."** Taken literally, this means switching GPIO25 on and starting the conversion at the same moment. That leaves out the ~20 ms wait that `ads1115-dev/DESIGN.md:37` requires between switch-on and the first conversion.
   - **Why a wait is needed:** while GPIO25 is low, the 100 nF capacitors on A0 and A1 sit at 0 V. At switch-on they charge through the resistance in front of them, so each input climbs to its final voltage instead of stepping there.
   - **How fast the inputs climb:** for A1 the resistance is the 1 kΩ plus the divider's own 10k‖R_th, so the time constant is 0.6 ms at 25 °C, 0.87 ms at 0 °C and 1.0 ms at −20 °C. For A0 it is only the 1 kΩ, so about 0.1 ms.
   - **What goes wrong without the wait:** an 8 SPS conversion averages its input over 125 ms, so it averages the climb in. The input reads low by about time constant ÷ 125 ms: 0.5% at 25 °C, 0.7% at 0 °C, 0.8% at −20 °C. Only the first conversion after switch-on is affected; by the second, the inputs have settled.
   - **Size in temperature, if A1 (the tap) is converted first:** the tap reads low, so the thermistor's resistance comes out low and the temperature reads warm. That is about +0.2 °C at 25 °C, +0.6 °C at 0 °C and +1.4 °C at −20 °C. The error grows in the cold, so it would look like a real temperature effect rather than an offset.
   - **If A0 is converted first:** A0 settles faster, and A1 has settled by the time it is read. The error is smaller and reversed: about −0.04 °C at 25 °C, −0.15 °C at −20 °C. That is still not negligible, so the order of the inputs alone is not a fix.
   - **The fix:** wait after switching GPIO25 on before the first conversion. Full settling to one ADC step takes about 11 time constants, so about 7 ms at 25 °C and about 11 ms at −20 °C. DESIGN.md's ~20 ms leaves margin. Discarding the first conversion also works, but costs 125 ms of extra excitation. Either way the excitation stays on longer, which adds to the self-heating in item 4.
   - **How sure:** these figures assume the conversion weights its 125 ms evenly. The ADS1115's actual filter weighting isn't known here, which probably explains why DESIGN.md:38 gives about 0.15 °C at room temperature where this estimate gives about 0.2 °C. DESIGN.md's planned bench test (vary the wait until the reading stops changing) settles it either way.
   - **Wording for Table 1:** "GPIO25 high from about 20 ms before the first conversion until the last conversion ends."

2. **Wrong: FIRMWARE.md, "Conversions run between transmissions, so they avoid transmit bursts."** The firmware doesn't control all radio activity:
   - In power-save the station wakes for every DTIM beacon (every 100–300 ms), so 125 ms conversions will overlap wakes.
   - `sendto()` returns before the frame actually goes out, and MAC-level retries follow later.
   - The median-of-3–5 row admits this, so the doc contradicts itself.
   - Power-save also adds a periodic current step (~20 → ~100 mA) at the beacon rate. With power-save off, the current is a steadier ~100 mA receive load. So MIN_MODEM is a power/heat choice, not an EMI fix as Table 2 implies.

3. **Wrong: FIRMWARE.md, "no WiFi power-save setting" and the open item "Set WiFi power-save".** `WIFI_PS_MIN_MODEM` is already the ESP-IDF default (`esp-idf/components/esp_wifi/include/esp_wifi.h:721`). `common/wifi/wifi.c` never changes it, so the firmware already runs in that mode. The planned "WiFi idle" noise test will therefore run with power-save on.

4. **Wrong: WIRING.md:361, "pulsed excitation keeps self-heating to a few thousandths of a degree."**
   - Continuous heating is 0.27 mW → 0.136 °C, so the doc's 0.14 is right.
   - With the planned sampling (3–5 readings × 2 channels at 8 SPS), the excitation is on 0.75–1.25 s. That gives 0.02–0.03 °C at the end of the pulse with τ = 5 s, and a shorter τ makes it worse.
   - Only a pulse as short as one A0+A1 pair (~0.25 s) keeps the heating near 0.007 °C.
   - DESIGN.md:41 lists this as "still to discuss", so WIRING states as fact what is still open.

5. **Contradiction: WIRING.md:484, "a thermistor leak just moves the temperature by a few tenths of a degree."** Line 470 says films can fall to tens of kΩ. A 50 kΩ leak at 25 °C is about 20% → ~4.5 °C. Even a 1 MΩ leak gives +1.6 °C at −20 °C, the conditions where fog and frost happen. Table 20 should add a cold case.

6. **Wrong: WIRING.md:485, "the warm box and the ADS1115 box are warmer than the air … so their films dry sooner."**
   - The ADS1115 box dissipates about 1 mW, so it sits at ambient.
   - It holds the tap node, the 10k and A1, which are exactly as leak-sensitive as the thermistor pads.
   - It should be grouped with the sensor end as needing protection, not with the warm box.

## Medium

7. **Unverified: the excitation runs on the noisy rail.** GPIO25 runs off the ESP32 board's own LDO, which also carries the WiFi current. The ADS1115 runs on the separate 3.3 V regulator (WIRING.md:95).
   - A0 and A1 are converted one after the other, so the ratio cancels only excitation error that stays constant between the two conversions. Converting A0, A1, A0 and averaging the two A0 readings would help.
   - A0 sits at about the ADS1115's VDD. If the ESP32 rail is higher than the bus rail, A0 is outside the specified input range (GND to VDD). Measure both rails.

8. **Contradiction about the I2C pull-ups.** Line 49 and the `main.c` header say the bus uses the ESP32's internal pull-ups. Table 20 says "the pull-ups are low impedance".
   - With ~45 kΩ internal pull-ups only, rise time at ~150–200 pF would be ~6–8 µs, far over the 1 µs limit at 100 kHz. The bus works, so the breakout boards' pull-ups must be what carries it. Line 187's "so the 100 kHz clock stays" depends on them, not on the 400 pF limit.
   - The leak threshold depends on this too. With ~2 kΩ total (typical 10k on the breakouts plus 4.7k on the DS3231 module, all unverified), it takes a leak of ~4 kΩ or less to break the high level. With internal pull-ups only, ~100 kΩ would. "Tens of kΩ" fits neither.
   - Line 413: checking idle levels won't reveal pull-up strength. Measure the resistance from SDA/SCL to 3.3 V with the power off.

9. **Imprecise: Table 20, divider tap row.**
   - "The tap sits at 10 to 33 kΩ" describes the thermistor, not the tap. The tap's source impedance is 10k‖R_th, about 5–7.7 kΩ.
   - A tap-to-GND leak is exactly the thermistor case. A leak to a neighbor at a high voltage (3.3 V, SDA, SCL or the energize node) is roughly −0.2 °C per MΩ at every temperature: −0.19 °C at 0 °C, against +0.63 °C for a GND leak. So it is not symmetric.
   - At jack 2, pin 7's only neighbors are pins 6 and 8, both ground. A leak at the jack therefore always reads warmer.
   - The "GND trace between them" option (line 487) puts a ground conductor closer to the tap than the 3.3 V trace was, which makes the worse kind of leak more likely. Spacing is the better option.

10. **Incomplete: the ADC-loading correction, "about 0.2% … about 0.045 °C" (line 365).** That figure holds at 25 °C only. Assuming ~5 MΩ input impedance (unverified, from memory), it is 0.65% / 0.13 °C at 0 °C and 1.9% / 0.33 °C at −20 °C. The correction must use the temperature-dependent value.

11. **Unverified: USB power path.** On most DevKit boards, USB 5 V reaches VIN through a diode, so the 5 V bus is about 4.3–4.7 V on USB. Whether the 3.3 V regulator's dropout allows that depends on its part number.

## Low

12. **Wrong, minor: CAT5 #1 capacitance.** 2 ft at 50 pF/m is ~30 pF, not 20. The conclusion still holds.
13. **Guess stated as fact: line 400.** The 0.6 °C offset is attributed to sensor location. The BMP388's ±0.5 °C temperature accuracy (from memory) explains it on its own.
14. **Unexplained: energize row, "only a leak of about 1 kΩ or less matters".** A0 senses the same node as the top of the divider, so any steady leak cancels in the ratio, as long as the leak current stays within what the GPIO can drive.
15. **Wording: DS3231 battery row, "Clock loses time".** A drained cell more likely shows as the oscillator-stop flag being set or the time resetting.
16. **Wording: Table 19 and the `main.c` header, "range the part can report".** The code checks the specified operating range, which is narrower.

## sensor-probe/main/main.c

All of the following match my knowledge of the datasheets:
- **Registers:** 0x00, 0x02, 0x03, 0x04–0x09, 0x1B and 0x7E.
- **Mode, status and error bits:** forced mode 0x13, status ready mask 0x60, error mask 0x07.
- **Calibration:** 21 bytes from 0x31 to 0x45, with all 14 signed/unsigned types and power-of-two scalings correct, including (P1 − 2^14)/2^20, (P2 − 2^14)/2^29 and P11/2^65.
- **Compensation:** the t_lin and pressure formulas, and the raw byte order.
- **SHT45:** command 0xFD, 8.3 ms maximum, CRC-8 (poly 0x31, start 0xFF), both conversions, and the clamping.

The CRC on Sensirion's example input 0xBEEF gives 0x92, as expected. Both `goto out` paths are clean: `dev` is never NULL at `out`, and jumping past the `double` declarations is legal C. The tick reasoning is right too: `vTaskDelay(2)` waits at least 10 ms.

- **Suggestion (medium): put the BMP388 in a known state first.** The code never soft-resets it and relies on the reset defaults. The OSR reset value of 0x02 (×4 pressure, ×1 temperature) gives a ~11 ms conversion (both figures from memory). The BMP388 stays powered across an ESP32 EN reset, so any OSR or IIR settings left by `ambient` or another firmware carry over. At ×32/×2 a conversion takes ~70 ms, longer than the 60 ms wait, which gives a false "not ready" FAIL. `BMP388_REG_CMD` and `BMP388_CMD_SOFT_RESET` are defined but never used. Soft-reset at the start and poll the ready bits with a deadline.
- **Suggestion (low): accept chip id 0x60.** A BMP390 uses the same compensation, and DESIGN.md:80 already expects relabelled parts.
- **Unverified:** the "section 9.1" reference in the comment, and how ESP-IDF 6.1's I2C master recovers after a NACK.

## Corrections after checking sources (2026-10-26)

These replace figures above that were recalled from memory.

- **BMP388 measurement time:** Bosch's BMP3 driver (`bmp3_defs.h`, `bmp3.c`) uses 234 µs, plus 392 µs and 2000 µs per oversampling step for pressure, plus 313 µs and 2000 µs per step for temperature. The review's 163 µs and 2020 µs were wrong. The conclusion stands: at ×32 pressure and ×2 temperature a measurement takes about 69 ms, longer than the old 60 ms wait. The driver also confirms the registers, chip ids (0x50 for the BMP388, 0x60 for the BMP390), the calibration layout and scalings, the compensation formulas, and the soft reset (0xB6 to 0x7E, then 2 ms).
- **ADS1115 input impedance:** the TI datasheet gives 6 MΩ common-mode and 15 MΩ differential at ±4.096 V. With A1 measured against ground the two may act in parallel, about 4.3 MΩ (not checked against the datasheet's input model). The review's "about 5 MΩ" is within that range, so item 10's figures roughly stand.
- **SHT45 accuracy:** ±0.1 °C is the typical figure, stated for about 0 to 75 °C (Sensirion product page and SHT4x datasheet table, as reported by search; the datasheet figure for the maximum error was not checked).

## Status (2026-10-26)

Item 7 is fixed by reading A0 through a divider of two equal 10k 0.1% resistors (the author has these on hand), so A0 stays inside the ADS1115's input range however the ESP32's 3.3 V moves. The excitation can still change between the A0 and A1 readings; reading A0, A1, A0 and averaging the two A0 readings handles that, in the ADS1115 firmware.

Item 11 is closed: the author checked that the ESP32 board has no diode between USB 5 V and VIN, so the 5 V bus gets the full USB voltage.

Item 13 is closed: the BMP388's temperature is the warm box's temperature, so it isn't expected to match the SHT45. The BMP388 is there for pressure.

Items 2 and 4 are closed: the author chose one A0, A1, A0 set per reading, with no median. The excitation is on for about 0.4 s at 8 SPS, which heats the thermistor about 0.006 °C (time constant 5 s and dissipation constant 2 mW/°C, read off the Littelfuse JL datasheet by the author). The claim that conversions avoid transmit bursts is removed.

Item 8 is closed without a measurement: the docs now say the breakout boards' pull-ups carry the bus, and Table 20 no longer gives a leak threshold for I2C.

Fixed in the docs and code: items 1 to 16, and both `main.c` suggestions (soft reset with ready-bit polling, and BMP390 chip id). `sensor-probe` builds; it has not been flashed and run since the change.

Nothing is still open.
